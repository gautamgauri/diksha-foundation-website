Poonji

Many women we meet are already working: running a small shop, stitching clothes, selling vegetables or preparing snacks. What is missing is often one practical thing.

Poonji begins with a visit and a conversation. The woman decides what work makes sense for her. Diksha helps with equipment, financial literacy, mentoring and follow-up.

The work

The woman chooses the livelihood.

We do not arrive with one business idea for everyone. A useful plan has to fit the woman’s skills, her family responsibilities, the neighbourhood and the customers she can reach.

01

It starts at home.

The team visits households and listens. We ask about the work a woman already does, what she earns, what gets in the way and what she would like to try.

02

Support is based on the actual need.

That may mean a sewing machine, a wooden cart, a refrigerator or material for a small shop. Earlier groups also used small grants and credit through their own bank accounts.

03

Money and business are discussed plainly.

Meetings cover saving, pricing, customers, stock and basic financial decisions. Women can bring questions from the work they are already doing.

04

The equipment is not the end of the relationship.

Follow-up visits and mentoring help the team understand what is working, where a business is stuck and whether a different kind of support is needed.

One current example

Samanti Devi already had the shop.

Samanti Devi is 59 and lives in Haj Bhawan. After her husband’s death, the small shop she runs became central to how she supports herself.

When the Poonji team met her, she did not ask us to invent a business. She explained what the shop was missing: a refrigerator. It would let her stock milk, curd, lassi and cold drinks alongside the goods she already sold.

Poonji provided the refrigerator. The business remained hers—the idea, the daily work and the decisions. Diksha supplied one tool that made the next step possible.

Why we are sharing this example

The request came from Samanti Devi herself. This account comes from the March 2026 Poonji CSR record. We do not publish an income figure because the current record does not verify one.

Poonji over time

Poonji has used different models over the years.

Poonji began during the Covid years, when women’s small businesses and household incomes were under pressure. It has moved between group enterprises and individual, need-based support.

2021–2023

Fifteen self-help groups

Programme records describe 100 women across 12 slums. Their work included vegetable vending, ration shops, tailoring and beauty services.

2023–2024

Skills and market links

Training plans included catering, handicrafts, sales and support for existing businesses in four Patna neighbourhoods.

2024–2025

Work in Phulwari Sharif

Fifteen women formed the Sui-Dhaga and Beauty Women’s groups. They opened bank accounts and began saving.

2025–2026

Individual livelihood support

Fifty-six women received equipment and mentoring for tailoring, vending, food stalls and small shops.

A lesson from Phulwari Sharif

The survey did not produce a neat answer.

In 2024, Poonji met women sanitation workers in Phulwari Sharif. Many said they preferred secure municipal employment to starting a group business. Others wanted to try tailoring or beauty work.

We did not judge one choice as better than the other. The team continued outreach and worked with the women who wanted to form enterprises. By December, two groups with 15 members had begun work.

Where Poonji stands now

Poonji is active in FY 2026–27.

eGain Communications renewed its support in March 2026. The current workplan continues equipment support, fortnightly meetings and business mentoring in Patna.
