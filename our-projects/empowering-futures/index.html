Empowering Futures

Twice a week, girls from six Patna neighbourhoods meet in small groups close to home. They talk, play football, make things, perform and learn from one another.

Empowering Futures works with girls aged 13–18 in Shastri Nagar, Haj Bhawan, China Kothi, Bhola Paswan, Mahuabagh and Kaushal Nagar. Each girl gets room to speak and make choices. Our team also meets families and connects the groups with local public services.

Empowering Futures programme records, January–March 2026.

Twice a week

The girls meet in familiar groups close to home.

Each neighbourhood has two peer groups. The girls meet the same facilitators and one another regularly. As the same girls return, questions become easier to ask and the room begins to feel like their own.

01

Speaking and making choices

Through conversation, journals and role-play, girls put their experiences into words. They talk about school, work and the choices in front of them.

02

Health, bodies and safety

Girls can ask direct questions about menstruation, their bodies, consent, personal safety and online safety. They also learn where to go when they need help.

03

Rights and public services

The group learns about scholarships, government schemes and health services—and the forms, documents and offices that can otherwise make these difficult to use.

04

Football and teamwork

Football brings girls onto the field. They practise, build a team, take responsibility for one another and become more at ease in a public space.

05

Making, performing and telling stories

A ten-day theatre workshop ended in a public performance. In art and filmmaking too, girls decide what they want to make and which stories they want to tell.

What we look for

More choices in everyday life.

At Diksha, we do not look only at attendance or the information covered in a session. We ask a more practical question: what can a girl now do that was harder before? It may be asking a question, travelling somewhere new, seeking help or making a decision about her studies. This is how the capabilities approach becomes part of our everyday work.

01

I can think and choose

I can imagine a future, understand my options and make a more informed decision.

02

I can move with confidence

I understand my body and safety. I can play, travel and become familiar with more of the city around me.

03

My voice matters

I can ask a question, tell a story, perform and speak before a group in my own voice.

04

I belong and can lead

I can build friendships, stand by another girl and take responsibility for something we are doing together.

05

I can ask for what I need

I know more about my rights and can approach a health service, fill a form or ask an institution for help.

Learning beyond the neighbourhood

Twenty-five girls went to Bihta. For some, it was their first exhibition.

In December 2025, girls from all six neighbourhoods travelled to KHEL Bihta for Matrix Mela.

Children at the centre were explaining mathematics through models and games. The girls tried the activities themselves and visited Sitaram Ashram, where they learnt about Swami Sahajanand Saraswati.

Some had never attended an exhibition like this before. They were soon handling the models, asking questions and watching other young people explain work they had made.

Around each peer group

We work with the people around each girl.

Our facilitators stay in touch with families, schools, Anganwadi centres, Urban Primary Health Centres, ASHA workers and community leaders. A girl needs people around her who will listen and help when she takes a new step.

Health and safety

Health care, closer to home.

At community health camps, girls and women can speak directly with health workers. The questions continue in regular sessions on body awareness, health and personal safety.

Rights and institutions

Knowing where to go.

Civic-rights sessions help girls and families understand which public office does what, what support they can ask for and what they can do when access is difficult.

The first year

A year of turning up, week after week.

Trust grew through home visits, conversations with families and girls returning to the same groups. From there, the work moved onto football fields, stages, exhibitions and visits across Patna.

October–December 2025

Beginning with families and neighbourhoods

Facilitators visit homes, meet families and bring the first peer groups together. By December, 260 girls are taking part. The programme holds its public launch and its first visit to KHEL Bihta.

January–March 2026

Twelve groups find their rhythm

Active participation reaches 280 girls. Twelve peer groups are meeting twice a week, and sport becomes a regular part of the programme. Baseline and community-mapping work is also completed.

March–July 2026

Girls perform and exhibit their work

Girls perform after a ten-day theatre workshop, present their work at the Mitti Se exhibition, join civic-rights and health sessions, and begin learning storytelling and filmmaking.

Empowering Futures runs from 2025 to 2028 with support from Azim Premji Foundation. Education continuity, access to schemes and peer leadership will be tracked over the full three-year period.
