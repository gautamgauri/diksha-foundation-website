# Diksha Foundation website instructions

## Public-facing writing

These rules apply to every page, heading, caption, call to action, metadata description, alt text, and other public-facing text in this repository.

1. **Use the appropriate voice skill before drafting or substantially revising text.** For Diksha website copy, use the approved Diksha brand-voice guidance and Brand Identity Prism. Do not rely on a general writing style or on the cadence of existing generated copy. If the relevant voice skill or approved brand guidance is unavailable, pause and obtain it before creating final copy.
2. **Keep the evidence boundary intact.** Source records determine facts, dates, numbers, programme status, and outcomes. Voice guidance changes expression, not evidence. Clearly distinguish completed work, current activity, plans, and targets.
3. **Write from Diksha's point of view.** The language should be warm, conversational, specific, locally grounded, and attentive to relationships. Children, young people, women, families, and communities appear as capable participants. Diksha enables, accompanies, listens, and creates space; it is not the hero of their lives.
4. **Prefer lived detail to programme language.** Use real places, people, activities, choices, questions, and observed moments when the sources support them. Use direct verbs and ordinary words. Explain frameworks through what people actually do and experience.
5. **Complete a separate anti-LLM-rhythm edit before considering copy finished.** Read the full page aloud or scan it as continuous prose, not section by section. Remove or rewrite:
   - repeated three-part or four-part lists;
   - successive sentences with the same length or grammatical shape;
   - neat binary contrasts such as “not only X, but Y” or “the setting changed; the belief did not”;
   - slogan-like fragments and artificial one-line declarations;
   - abstract NGO language such as “journey,” “transformation,” “empowerment,” “unlocking potential,” “creating impact,” and “a doorway to opportunity,” unless a source or context genuinely requires the term;
   - metaphorical, symmetrical, or overly polished headings that could belong to any organisation;
   - generic claims that could be pasted onto another education or development website.
6. **Let the cadence remain human.** Vary sentence length naturally. Do not force every paragraph into a setup, balanced list, and concluding uplift. A plain factual sentence is often better than a polished line.
7. **Do not approve copy on factual validation alone.** Final review must confirm both source accuracy and voice. When reporting completion, state that the appropriate voice guidance was used and that the anti-LLM-rhythm pass was completed.

Existing text is not automatically a voice reference. Reuse it only after checking it against these rules.
