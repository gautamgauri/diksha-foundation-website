Holistic education

We begin with what a child can do.

At KHEL, educators know each learner and adjust the work as the child grows. This takes time.

01 · The whole child

School results are one part of the plan.

Children need to read, write and understand mathematics. They also need to speak, make decisions, work with other people and discover what they care about.

The capabilities approach gives us a practical question: what is this child increasingly able to be and do? Can the child explain an idea to someone else? Will they stay with a piece of work until it is finished? In a group, do they listen and contribute? These questions sit beside attendance, schoolwork and assessment.

01

Academic foundations

Children work on language, mathematics and the ideas they encounter at school.

02

Social & emotional learning

They learn to understand their feelings, listen to others and work through difficulty.

03

STEAM & digital agency

Children ask questions, make things, experiment and learn to use technology for a purpose.

04

Arts & creativity

Music, theatre and visual art give children different ways to think and express themselves.

05

Civic leadership

Bal Sansad and group work give children practice in speaking, deciding and taking responsibility.

06

Health & wellbeing

Food, movement, safety and care for the natural world are part of the learning environment.

What children should experience

Freedom, joy and democracy

01

Freedom

Children can ask why, pursue an interest and make choices about their work.

02

Joy

There is time for play, art, discovery and friendship, including on an ordinary day.

03

Democracy

Children speak in meetings, listen to other views and take part in decisions.

02 · The three Rs

Relationships, responsibility and reverence

These are expectations for the adults at Diksha as much as they are for children.

Relationships

Educators know the children and families they work with. Trust is built over repeated, ordinary contact.

Responsibility

Children help care for the centre, keep commitments to a group and take ownership of work they begin.

Reverence for all life

Children are asked to treat other people, themselves and the natural world with dignity and care.
