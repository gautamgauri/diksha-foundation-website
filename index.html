01 · Why Diksha exists

Learning has never been confined to the classroom.

Many children first come to KHEL because they want help with school. They find books, teachers and computers, along with people who have time to listen and explain something again.

As they settle in, children join a football team, prepare a play, make work for an exhibition or represent their group in Bal Sansad. They take part from the beginning and get more chances to find out what they can do.

02 · What children experience

A day at KHEL moves between schoolwork and other kinds of learning.

Schoolwork is part of the timetable. So are projects, performances, conversations, sport and the everyday work of sharing a centre with other people.

01

Academic foundations

Reading, writing and maths, with time to practise and ask again.

02

Social and emotional growth

Learning to understand oneself, listen and work through difficulty.

03

STEAM and digital agency

Making, questioning, coding and using technology with purpose.

04

Arts and expression

Music, theatre, dance and visual art, including work made for an audience.

05

Citizenship and leadership

Bal Sansad, shared decisions, rights and responsibility.

06

Health and wellbeing

Food, movement, sport, safety and care for the living world.

What children achieve

Children present some of their work in public.

At these events, children present work to families, visitors and one another. They have to finish what they started, explain it clearly and respond when someone asks a question.

Theatre & dance

Theatre and dance are performed before an audience.

Children rehearse together and are responsible for being ready when the performance begins.

Mitti Se

Students organise an exhibition.

They made the work, arranged the arts-and-crafts exhibition and explained it to visitors.

Matrix Mela

The maths games are made by children.

They designed maths games, set up the mela and taught families and visitors how to play.

Diksha Premier League

The teams play to win.

Patna Pythons, Beta Blues, Futures FC and RJ Champions train and play a full tournament.

Long-term presence

Some relationships at Diksha now span years.

Children and families come to know the educators, and the educators come to know them.

Children grow up with the centre and take on more responsibility as they do. Some former KHEL students have returned to Diksha as educators and mentors. Younger children now see people they know doing that work.

Badi Maa · 1956–2019

Badi Maa was at the centre from 2010 to 2019.

From 2010 to 2019, Badi Maa volunteered full time with Diksha.

She came to the centre every day and worked alongside children and educators through Diksha’s first nine years.

Children remember her care, simplicity and joy. They remember her as part of the ordinary day at the centre.

Nine years of daily work shaped what Diksha became. We remember Badi Maa through the care given to children at the centre.
